# Moore's Law Explained Simply

**Moore's Law** is an observation made by Intel co-founder Gordon Moore in 1965. He noticed that the number of transistors (tiny switches) that could fit on a computer chip roughly **doubled every two years**, while the cost stayed about the same.

In simple terms: **Computer chips get twice as powerful (or twice as cheap) roughly every two years.**

This isn't a law of physics—it's more like a trend/prediction that the tech industry worked hard to keep true for decades, driving massive improvements in computing power, smartphones, and the internet we use today.

## Summary Table

| Aspect | Description |
|---|---|
| **Who proposed it** | Gordon Moore (Intel co-founder), 1965 |
| **Core idea** | Transistors on a chip double approximately every 2 years |
| **Effect** | Computing power increases, cost per transistor decreases |
| **Type** | Empirical observation/trend, not a physical law |
| **Real-world impact** | Enabled smartphones, laptops, AI, internet growth |
| **Current status** | Slowing down due to physical limits (atoms are only so small) |
| **Why it mattered** | Guided industry goals & investment for 50+ years |
