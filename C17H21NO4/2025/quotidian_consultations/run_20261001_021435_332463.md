# Moore's Law Explained Simply

**The Core Idea:** Every 2 years, the number of transistors (tiny switches) that fit on a computer chip roughly *doubles* — making computers faster, cheaper, and smaller over time.

Think of it like this: If you could fold a piece of paper 50 times, it would be thick enough to reach the moon. That's the power of doubling!

## Visual: The Doubling Effect

```
Year 2000: 🔲 (1 million transistors)
Year 2002: 🔲🔲 (2 million)
Year 2004: 🔲🔲🔲🔲 (4 million)
Year 2006: 🔲🔲🔲🔲🔲🔲🔲🔲 (8 million)
...and so on, doubling each time
```

The line *curves upward* dramatically — this is called **exponential growth**, unlike a straight line (linear growth).

## Real-World Analogy

Imagine a car that doubled its speed every 2 years since 1970:
- 1970: 10 mph 🚗
- 2024: Over 160,000 mph 🚀

That's roughly how much computing power has grown!

## Why It Matters

- 📱 Smartphones today are more powerful than room-sized supercomputers from the 1970s
- 💰 Costs per transistor keep dropping
- ⚡ This drove the digital revolution (internet, AI, smartphones)

## 📊 Summary Table

| Aspect | Description |
|---|---|
| **Who coined it?** | Gordon Moore, Intel co-founder (1965) |
| **What it predicts** | Transistors on a chip double approximately every 2 years |
| **Type of growth** | Exponential (not linear) |
| **Effect on performance** | Computing power increases significantly |
| **Effect on cost** | Cost per transistor decreases |
| **Effect on size** | Chips get smaller/more compact |
| **Is it still true?** | Slowing down since ~2010s due to physical limits (atoms are only so small!) |
| **Why it matters** | Enabled smartphones, AI, internet, modern computing |
| **Not a law of physics** | It's an *observation/trend*, not a guaranteed law |
