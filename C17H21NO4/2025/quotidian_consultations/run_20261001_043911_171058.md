# Moore's Law Explained Simply

## The Idea
In 1965, Intel co-founder **Gordon Moore** observed that the number of **transistors** (tiny electronic switches) on a computer chip **doubles roughly every two years**. Computers get faster, smaller, and cheaper at an exponential pace.

## Everyday Analogy
Imagine a city where the number of homes doubles every two years. Start with 1,000 homes:

- Year 0: 1,000
- Year 2: 2,000
- Year 4: 4,000
- Year 10: 32,000
- Year 20: 1,024,000

Growth looks slow at first, then explodes.

## Why It Matters
- Smartphones today outperform room-sized supercomputers of the 1970s.
- Enabled AI, graphics, and the internet.

## Limits
- Transistors are approaching atomic size.
- Heat and manufacturing costs are rising.
- Progress is slowing, with the industry shifting toward specialized chips (GPUs, AI accelerators) and 3D stacking.

## Summary Table

| Key Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Who** | Gordon Moore, 1965 (revised 1975) |
| **What** | Transistors per chip double about every 2 years |
| **Type of growth** | Exponential |
| **Result** | Faster, cheaper, smaller electronics |
| **Example** | 2,300 transistors (1971) → tens of billions (2020s) |
| **Nature** | Observation/trend, not a physical law |
| **Challenges** | Atomic limits, heat, cost |
| **Future** | Specialized chips, chip stacking, new materials, quantum computing |
