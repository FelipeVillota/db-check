# Moore's Law Explained Simply

## The Idea
In 1965, Intel co-founder **Gordon Moore** observed that the number of **transistors** (tiny electronic switches) on a computer chip **doubles roughly every two years**. Chips get more powerful, and cost per unit of computing falls.

## Everyday Analogy
Imagine a city where the number of houses doubles every two years on the same plot of land. To fit them, every house must shrink. Chips work the same way: transistors keep getting smaller so more fit on one chip.

## Simple Visualization

Transistors per chip (approximate, log-style growth):

Each step is **vastly** bigger than the last, a pattern called **exponential growth**:

```
Power
  |                          *
  |                       *
  |                    *
  |               *
  |        *
  |  *  *
  +---------------------------- Time
```

## Real-World Impact
- **1971 Intel 4004:** 2,300 transistors
- **Today's chips (e.g., Apple M-series, Nvidia GPUs):** tens to hundreds of billions
- Your smartphone is more powerful than room-sized 1980s supercomputers.

## Is It Still True?
It's **slowing down**. Transistors are now only a few atoms wide, so physics (heat, quantum effects) and rising costs limit shrinking. The industry now relies on new approaches: 3D stacking, specialized chips (AI accelerators), and chiplets.

## Summary Table

| Key Point | Details |
|---|---|
| **Who** | Gordon Moore, 1965 (revised 1975) |
| **Observation** | Transistors per chip double about every 2 years |
| **Type of growth** | Exponential |
| **Not a law of physics** | An industry trend and self-fulfilling goal |
| **Benefits** | Faster, cheaper, smaller, more efficient devices |
| **Example** | 2,300 transistors (1971) → 100+ billion (2020s) |
| **Current status** | Slowing due to atomic-scale limits and cost |
| **Future paths** | 3D chips, chiplets, AI-specific hardware, quantum computing |

**Bottom line:** Moore's Law is the reason technology has become dramatically more powerful and affordable over the past 50+ years, though its pace is now fading.
