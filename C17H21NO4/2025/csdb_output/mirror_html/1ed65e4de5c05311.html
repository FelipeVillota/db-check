WATCH: Aerial fire ends nearly 400 pound cocaine smuggling attempt near Puerto Rico

by Grace Bellinghausen

SAN JUAN, Puerto Rico (CBS12) — U.S. Customs and Border Protection's Air and Marine Operations used air disabling fire to stop a drug smuggling boat carrying nearly 400 pounds of cocaine near Puerto Rico.

In a statement released Friday, officials say an air crew first spotted a suspicious 25-foot blue vessel northwest of the island on May 13. The boat had three people onboard and visible packages, prompting CBP to track it overnight.

The following night, federal marine units moved in with interceptor boats and aircraft. Officials say the vessel attempted to flee, and those onboard began throwing packages into the water.

A CBP Black Hawk crew then used air-disabling fire to stop the boat north of San Juan.

Agents boarded the vessel and took three Dominican Republic nationals into custody. They also recovered five bales of cocaine weighing about 391 pounds, along with electronic devices.

CBP Air and Marine Operations says the case underscores a broader Caribbean trafficking corridor that continues to feed drug flows toward the United States, with South Florida serving as a major entry and distribution hub.

Federal data shows large cocaine seizures continue in and around Florida waters. On Friday, authorities say they seized about 900 pounds of cocaine worth an estimated $6.7 million in Miami. In early May, nearly two tons of cocaine were also intercepted off South Florida’s coast, according to previous reports.

The seized drugs and suspects were turned over to Homeland Security Task Force investigators.
